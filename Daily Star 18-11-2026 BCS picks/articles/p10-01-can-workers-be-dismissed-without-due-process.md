# Can workers be dismissed without due process?

_KAZI MUHAMMAD ELIAS — Law & Our Rights — PDF p10_

![](../images/p10_03.jpg)

Workers are often removed from their jobs on the grounds of alleged misconduct without following due process. However, many workers fail to get a remedy as they do not know where and when to file their grievance. Sections 23 and 24 of the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006 (the ‘BLA’), read with Rule 29 of the Bangladesh Labour Rules, 2015, govern the procedure for imposing punishment, including the dismissal of workers from a company. Section 23(1) of the BLA provides that a worker may be dismissed from service if he is found guilty of misconduct under section 24. Section 23(2) further provides that, instead of dismissal under section 23(1), a worker found guilty of misconduct may receive other forms of punishment, including removal, demotion, suspension, or a fine.

Section 24(1) of the BLA prescribes certain conditions to be fulfilled before imposing punishment, i.e. the allegation must be in writing, a copy of such allegation shall be provided to the accused worker, at least seven days shall be given to reply to the allegation, an opportunity to be heard shall be given, and an enquiry committee shall be formed, consisting of an equal number of representatives from both employers and workers, to inquire into the allegation. Some companies in Bangladesh comply with all the conditions except the last one: they constitute the enquiry committee only with the representatives of their own. It renders the disciplinary proceedings legally defective and gives workers a substantial ground to challenge the order of removal before the Labour Court.

However, there is a key precondition before challenging the order of removal before the Labour Court, failing which the worker may be deprived of the remedy. The workers must first submit a ‘grievance petition’ to the employer within the statutory period. The process of submitting a grievance petition is described in section 33(1) of the BLA, which states that a worker aggrieved by an order of removal may file a grievance petition to the employer by registered post within 30 days of knowledge of such removal. But if the grievance is submitted directly to the employer, service by registered post is not required. Section 33(2) of the BLA says that the employer shall inquire into and dispose of the grievance petition within 30 (thirty) days of receipt of the grievance. On the other hand, if the workers fail to submit the grievance petition within 30 days, their direct claim before the Labour Court will be rejected.

Notably, the time to submit a grievance petition was previously 15 days under section 25 of the Employment of Labour (Standing Orders) Act, 1965 (this Act has been repealed by the BLA). Moreover, according to section 33(3) of the BLA, workers may file a complaint in the Labour Court if they are dissatisfied with the employer’s decision in disposing of the grievance petition or if the employer fails to dispose of the petition within 30 days of its receipt. However, such a complaint must be filed within 30 days next to the aforesaid period. A complaint filed before the Labour Court after expiry of 30 days is not maintainable. This position was illuminated in the case of the Deputy Managing Director, National Bank of Pakistan, Dacca v Kazi Fazlul Karim (1976), where the Court refused to grant remedy to the claimant as the case was filed before the Labour Court beyond the statutory limitation period.

In short, workers removed from any company on the ground of alleged misconduct should examine whether the company committed any procedural irregularities in taking such action. If any such irregularity is found, they should place their grievance petition before the appropriate authority within the statutory period. Otherwise, their claims may be rejected on procedural grounds without determining substantive merits.
