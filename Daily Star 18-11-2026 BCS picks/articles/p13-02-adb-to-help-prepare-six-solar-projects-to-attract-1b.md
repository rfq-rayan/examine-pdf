# ADB to help prepare six solar projects to attract $1b

_STAR BUSINESS REPORT — Star Business — PDF p13, p15_

The Asian Development Bank (ADB) will provide transaction advisory services for five additional solar and battery storage projects in Bangladesh, expanding its project pipeline to more than 1,000 megawatts (MW) across six projects.

The expanded programme builds on ADB’s advisory support for the 130MW Sonagazi solar power project in Feni, the multilateral development bank said in a press statement yesterday.

The programme is expected to mobilise more than $1 billion in private investment, as the government seeks to increase renewable energy generation and reduce dependence on imported fuels.

According to ADB, the programme will also support the government’s target of generating at least 20 percent of electricity from renewable sources by 2030 and 30 percent by 2040.

The statement, however, did not provide further details regarding the five additional projects.

### TRANSACTION ADVISORY SERVICE

ADB’s transaction advisory services will cover the entire project development cycle, including project preparation, technical and financial assessments, market sounding, preparation of tender documents, and assistance with competitive bidding and contract awards.

The services are intended to help the government structure commercially viable projects, attract private investors and select project partners through competitive procurement.

ADB’s role under the arrangement is advisory, rather than a commitment to finance or construct the power plants.

The tender for the Sonagazi project, which will include a battery energy storage system with a capacity of 26 megawatt-hours (MWh), is expected to be launched within this year.

ADB said the first project would serve as a model for structuring and procuring the remaining five projects under the programme’s second and third phases, with lessons from Sonagazi informing subsequent developments.

Under the expanded arrangement, ADB will assist Invest Bangladesh and the Bangladesh Power Development Board (BPDB) in assessing the suitability of government-owned land for the five additional projects.

The memorandum of understanding on the expanded programme was signed by Md Mostafa Kamal, member (additional secretary), Public Private Partnership (PPP) at Invest Bangladesh; Afroza Sultana, secretary of BPDB; and Qingfeng Zhang, ADB country director for Bangladesh.

“Bangladesh has an opportunity to build a more secure, sustainable, and investment-ready energy future,” Zhang said.

He said the expanded advisory services would help move beyond individual projects and create a scalable market for clean energy investment by strengthening project preparation, competitive procurement and the environment for private investment.

Ashik Chowdhury, chairman of Invest Bangladesh, said the government sought to accelerate private sector investment and participation, particularly foreign direct investment, in renewable energy projects.

He said the partnership with ADB would help expand the country’s clean energy capacity, diversify its energy mix and strengthen long-term energy security.
