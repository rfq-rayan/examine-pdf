*RMG wage board must act with urgency*

# Delays will only deepen workers’ frustration, undermine confidence in the process

_Editorial — PDF p6_

At a time when the country can least afford industrial unrest, let alone the risk of its political exploitation, we are perturbed by the unnecessary delay in wage negotiations in the RMG industry. The postponement of the wage board’s third meeting, reportedly on the grounds of a board member being ill, was clearly a mistake. Both the owners’ associations and workers’ unions have expressed dismay over the decision. More importantly, the relevant rules and regulations don’t require a meeting to be postponed simply because one member is absent.

Meanwhile, the continuing protests, which have turned violent on several occasions, reflect growing restlessness and frustration among workers over low wages and persistently high inflation. Economists have long argued that rising inflation has been eroding the value of annual wage increments, causing workers’ real wages to shrink at an alarming rate. Despite that, the government was not only late in constituting the wage board—which should have been formed well in advance to allow sufficient time for completing the wage revision process by November 2026—but also failed to engage with workers until they took to the streets about three weeks ago. Clearly, workers have begun to question its judgement and intentions, particularly given past experiences in which officials often appeared to side with factory owners.

The unnecessary deferment of the November 16 meeting, reportedly rescheduled for November 23, has done little to inspire their confidence in the process. Although the RMG sector’s remarkable growth and success have been driven largely by its women workforce, the absence of female representation on the board has further raised concerns about its ability to adequately understand and address workers’ needs. Further delays in wage negotiations will only fuel speculation and allow rumour mills to spread misinformation. Already, there are considerable misgivings about the owners’ yet-to-be-disclosed proposal for a new minimum wage.

It is also incumbent upon the government to ensure that ministers and advisers, as well as leaders of the ruling BNP and its allies, refrain from making arbitrary pronouncements. Such interventions risk creating the impression that the government cannot be trusted to act as a neutral arbiter in the wage negotiations. These politicians would do well to recall what minimum wage levels they themselves advocated when they were in opposition. We also urge the board to make every effort to bring all parties to the negotiating table at the earliest opportunity and sustain dialogue until an amicable settlement is reached. Similarly, we urge trade union leaders to encourage their members to exercise patience, allowing all parties sufficient time to find common ground.

It is true that the global economy is experiencing unprecedented turbulence amid multiple wars and conflicts, resulting in energy crises, supply chain disruptions, and rising commodity prices. At the same time, the country’s leading export industry must remain competitive in an increasingly challenging global market. This makes it all the more urgent to resolve the wage dispute without further delay and ensure a peaceful and stable industrial environment.
