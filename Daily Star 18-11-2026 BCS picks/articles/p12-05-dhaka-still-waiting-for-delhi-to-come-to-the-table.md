*GANGES TREATY EXPIRING ON DEC 12*

# Dhaka still waiting for Delhi to come to the table

_TASNIM MOHSIN and TASNEEM TAYEB — Law & Our Rights — PDF p12, p8_

With just 24 days left before the Ganges Water Sharing Treaty expires, Bangladesh has renewed its request to India to begin formal negotiations, following approaches in June and August that have yet to produce a timetable for talks.

Water Resources Minister Shahiduddin Chowdhury Anee recently wrote to his Indian counterpart, urging discussions without delay, according to a written statement shared with The Daily Star by Rashed Al Mahmud Titumir, adviser to the prime minister for finance and planning and head of Bangladesh’s technical committee on the treaty.

The statement said Dhaka informed New Delhi in June that it had formed a high-level technical committee and requested India to communicate the composition of its own team. Bangladesh repeated the request in August, asking India to constitute its committee and begin talks.

Bangladesh has completed all preparations and hopes India will take steps to initiate formal negotiations before the 30-year agreement expires on December 12, the briefing said.

Speaking on the condition of anonymity, a foreign ministry official told this newspaper, “Experts are suggesting an interim extension, but if we have to go for such an extension, India also has to agree.

“The executive level is ready for negotiation, but the decision has to come from the political level. And without the highest-level summit, this cannot be resolved,” the official said.

New Delhi has not publicly announced a date for formal negotiations or an interim water-sharing arrangement.

The uncertainty persists despite earlier expressions of optimism from officials on both sides.

On July 29, Indian High Commissioner Dinesh Trivedi said the countries’ working groups were discussing water-related issues and expressed hope that they would meet soon.

On August 9, he said a meeting between Prime Minister Tarique Rahman and his Indian counterpart Narendra Modi could help resolve bilateral differences. “I think if the two leaders meet, a lot of problems can be solved,” he told reporters.

However, Randhir Jaiswal, spokesperson for India’s external affairs ministry, on November 3 said he had no update to share on a proposed Joint Rivers Commission meeting.

Humayun Kabir, former Bangladesh ambassador to the US, told The Daily Star that a meeting between the two prime ministers could explore a variety of options.

“If the two prime ministers meet, they can agree in principle to either extend it for another term, or extend it for a short-term pending a new agreement, or even think of extending it for a year or two to review the same.”

Such an extension would allow technical teams to work out a longer-term agreement, he said. Alternatively, the governments could let the existing treaty expire and instruct negotiators under JRC to prepare a replacement within a specified period.

He described the treaty as a symbol of cooperation beyond water allocation.

“It is not only a technical agreement. It also has a huge political message,” he said, adding that he did not believe either government would want to lose the political significance the agreement carried for bilateral relations.

Meanwhile, India has held internal consultations on the Ganges treaty with representatives from Bihar and West Bengal, both of which have urged New Delhi to consider their water requirements in any renewed agreement.

Indian parliamentary records show that an inter-ministerial committee was formed in July 2023 to review the treaty. West Bengal participated in four meetings between October 2023 and March 2025, while Bihar attended four consultations between August 2023 and May 2024.

In August, Indian External Affairs Minister S Jaishankar told Janata Dal (United) MP Sanjay Kumar Jha, who also served as Bihar’s water resources minister from 2021 to 2024, that Bihar’s drinking-water and industrial requirements had been considered during internal consultations and would be taken into account in decisions on treaty renewal.

West Bengal has also sought adequate dry-season flows through the Bhagirathi- Hooghly system, citing concerns over navigation and port operations, according to a PTI report published on August 30.

In July, India’s Parliamentary Standing Committee on External Affairs urged New Delhi to complete its internal review and begin bilateral negotiations without further delay to avoid a gap in water-sharing arrangements after 2026, according to Indian media.

Signed in 1996, the Ganges Water Sharing Treaty allocates 35,000 cusecs of water to Bangladesh during each of three specified 10-day periods between March and May, with corresponding allocations for India. It also establishes joint monitoring arrangements at Farakka and Hardinge Bridge.

According to PM’s Adviser Titumir, the Ganges-dependent region covers roughly one-third of Bangladesh and is home to more than five crore people. Agriculture, fisheries, other economic activities, and ecosystems across the region, including the Sundarbans, depend heavily on the river’s water flows.

Earlier, Minister Anee said Bangladesh was seeking a new Ganges Water Sharing Treaty with India, rather than simply renewing the 1996 agreement, with a guarantee clause to ensure its due share of water during the dry season.

Ambassador Humayun Kabir said the expiry of the treaty without another arrangement would serve neither country.

“Our history, the last 55 years, tells us that this kind of situation is not helpful for either Bangladesh or India,” he said. “We have to have some kind of agreement as soon as possible before this one expires.”

Despite repeated requests, no comment could be obtained from the Indian High Commission in Dhaka until the filing of this report yesterday.
