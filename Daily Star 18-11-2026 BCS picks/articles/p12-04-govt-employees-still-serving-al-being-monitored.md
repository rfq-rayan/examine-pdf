# Govt employees still serving AL being monitored

**Says Adviser Zahed**

_STAFF CORRESPONDENT — Law & Our Rights — PDF p12, p9_

![](../images/p12_05.jpg)

The government is monitoring whether any serving government officials or employees remain involved with the Awami League, whose activities have been banned, said Zahed Ur Rahman, the prime minister’s adviser on information and broadcasting, yesterday.

Speaking at a weekly press briefing at the Press Information Department, he said, “We cannot disclose how the monitoring is being carried out, nor will we do so -- there are some state security issues involved.”

Zahed said many bureaucrats had been associated with the AL during its years in power, but their past political affiliations were not a concern.

“They are employees of the republic, and they are doing their jobs. But if they remain aligned with the party while serving in government positions that is a very serious matter. The government will certainly keep that in mind.”

Asked about a BBC report that India had set two conditions for ousted prime minister Sheikh Hasina’s return, Zahed said the government had no reason to respond without any official communication from another country.

He said Hasina was an accused person in Bangladesh and would face legal proceedings under Bangladeshi law if extradited.

Referring to an earlier statement by the law minister, Zahed said Hasina no longer had the right to appeal her death sentence. He also referred to a legal provision introduced by her father, Sheikh Mujibur Rahman, saying the government was following the same law.

He reiterated the government’s demand for Hasina’s extradition.

On the recent violence in Gopalganj, Zahed said those responsible would be identified, arrested and brought to justice.

Referring to the term “non-fugitive Awami League”, which has gained attention on social media, he warned that those acting at the instigation of fugitive AL leaders were putting themselves at risk.

Such activities constituted criminal offences, and those involved would be held accountable, he said.

Asked about the AL’s scheduled programme on November 19, Zahed said it was unlikely to have any significant impact in Dhaka or elsewhere.

He said law enforcement agencies would take necessary measures to maintain order, with police instructed to use proportionate force when necessary.

“The police have the right to use force -- they will operate in a way that is effective and ensures that the use of force is proportionate to the situation.”
